MusicD

MusicD Server

A simple music server for Sonos. It reads your music folders, shows your albums exactly as they are on disk, and plays them to your speakers — no metadata service, no album identification, no streaming accounts.

v0.4.25 Docker MIT licence Your files, your speakers

What it does

An album is a folder. Its title and artist come from the tags in it, and the folder name is the fallback. What the app keeps on top of that is a small database of your listening — because that is what the home screen is built out of.

📁Your library, as it is

Folder-based scanning with cover art taken from the folder or the tags. No title, artist, year or genre is ever fetched or matched against an online release, so nothing is renamed behind your back.

🔊Plays to Sonos

Rooms and groups are read from the speakers themselves. Playback goes through the Sonos queue, so tracks run gapless, and the audio streams straight from the server to the speaker with nothing transcoded in between.

📅Remembers what you play

Date added, date last played and play counts, for albums and tracks separately — counted by watching the speaker, so a queued album you skip past never counts.

✨Smart Picks, local only

Built from a taste profile of what you actually played in the last 90 days, and every pick tells you why it was chosen. Nothing leaves your network to build it.

🔍Search and browse

Albums, artists and tracks in one box, an artists index, and every home row opens into a full grid that pages as you scroll.

🗂️One album, however many copies

The album and its deluxe reissue are one card and one screen, with a tab for each version. The plain title is the album; the tabs swap the track list and the cover. The matching is local — artist, title without its edition marker, and the track names as a second opinion, so the four different albums called Weezer stay four albums.

🖼️Covers for albums that have none

An album with no picture in its folder and none in its tags gets one found for it, in the background, from MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive. No key and no account. Only the missing ones, only the image, and nothing is ever written next to your music. Switch it off with one tap, or for good with COVER_LOOKUP=false.

📻Scrobbles to Last.fm

Optional, and off until you connect an account. Every track the server counts as played is sent — the same half-way rule, so a skipped track is never scrobbled. Listens are saved before they are sent, so a reboot never loses one. Needs a free API key; see the installer.

📱Phone-first interface

The same palette and layout as MusicD Remote, in dark and light, with a mini transport on every screen and a Now Playing view with seek, volume and the live queue.

Install with Docker

Tell it where your music lives and which time zone you are in, then copy the command. Scrobbling to Last.fm needs a key, and there is a box for it below. The music mount is the one thing it cannot work without — everything else has a sensible default.

The absolute path on the host — the same path you would cd to. It is mounted read-only; the app never writes to your music.
Load-bearing here: “Not played in 6 months” is a calendar boundary and Smart Picks rebuild once a local day. On UTC both drift.
What you open in a browser. Change it only if something else on the host already owns 3400.
One is enough — the rest of the household is read from that player. Find it in the Sonos app under Settings → System → About My System.
Create one at last.fm/api/account/create. “MusicD Server” and your own address is a complete answer to every field.
Both are needed or neither works. They stay on this page — the shareable link this configurator writes into the address bar deliberately leaves them out, so a link you send somebody never carries your secret.
Then connect the account itself from the app's side menu: it opens Last.fm's own approval page, so MusicD never sees your password.
Only needed on a host with several network interfaces, where the wrong one might be picked.

The home screen

    
Seven rows, each a carousel that opens into a full grid when you tap its title.
       Hold the pad beside a row in the side menu and drag it to put them in your own order — the
       arrangement lives in the database, so every phone in the house agrees.

    

      

        
RowWhat is in it

        
          
FavouritesAlbums you tapped the heart on, most recently marked first.
              Absent rather than empty until there is one — a heading over nothing is an
              instruction to go and use a feature.

          
LibraryEvery album, by artist and then by year — the shelf order.

          
Random albumsA fresh handful every time the screen loads.

          
Recently addedNewest first, by the date the scan first saw the folder.

          
Recently playedMost recently played first.

          
Not played in 6 monthsAlbums you played longer than six months ago, and
              albums that have sat in the library that long unplayed. Longest gap first.
              Empty to begin with, and that is correct — nothing in a library scanned last
              week can have gone six months unplayed. It fills itself as time passes, which is why
              the time zone matters.

          
Smart PicksWhat you played in the last 90 days becomes a taste profile —
              artists, genres and decades, weighted so last week counts for more than March. Every
              album you have not played in the last 60 days is scored against it, and the
              best dozen are the picks, never two by the same artist. Each one says why it is
              there. An album with no connection to the profile is never offered: being unheard is
              a bonus, not a reason.

        
      

    

  






  

    
Environment

    
Everything is optional except your music path.

    

      

        
VariableDefaultPurpose

        
          
MUSIC_DIRS/musicFolders to scan inside the container. Separate several with commas.

          
TZEtc/UTCThe container's time zone. Set it — the six-month row and the daily Smart Picks rebuild are both local-time boundaries.

          
PORT3400Web interface, API and audio streaming.

          
DATA_DIR/app/dataDatabase and cached artwork.

          
SERVER_IPautoThe address given to speakers, for hosts with several interfaces.

          
SONOS_HOSTS—Player IPs, for when multicast discovery is unreliable. One is enough.

          
INCLUDE_ZONES—Show only these rooms, e.g. Kitchen,Study.

          
EXCLUDE_ZONES—Show everything except these rooms.

          
SCAN_ON_STARTtrueScan when the container starts.

          
SCAN_INTERVAL_HOURS6Hours between automatic rescans. 0 turns them off.

          
COVER_LOOKUPtrueLook online for covers albums do not have. false switches it off for good.

          
LASTFM_API_KEY—A Last.fm API key, from last.fm/api/account/create. Without one the Last.fm row does not appear.

          
LASTFM_API_SECRET—The shared secret that came with it. Both are needed or neither works.

What it deliberately does not do

    
These are decisions, not gaps. Your library never changes underneath you, and
       out of the box there is nothing to sign up for.

    

      
No metadata fetchingTitles, artists, years and genres come from your tags and
           nowhere else.

      
No album identificationNothing is matched against an online release, and no tag
           is ever rewritten.

      
No record labelsNo Discogs, no FanArt.tv.

      
No streaming servicesNo Qobuz, no TIDAL.

      
No reviews or scoresNo Pitchfork.

      
No wall displayThis one is a remote and a server, not a screen.

    

    
Two things do leave your network, and only these
       two. A missing cover is looked up at MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive —
       covers only, only for albums that have none, no key and no account, and
       COVER_LOOKUP=false stops it for good. And scrobbles go to Last.fm if you
       connect an account, which needs a key you supply and does nothing at all until you do.
       Neither one can change a single thing about your library.
